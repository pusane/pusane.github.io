Research interests

My research is in digital communications, error control coding, and signal analysis. The main areas are listed below, followed by funded projects and the research group.

Molecular communication

Diffusion channels, index modulation, microfluidic testbeds, and receivers built from engineered cells.

Error control coding

LDPC, spatially-coupled and polar codes; error floors, trapping sets, and low-complexity decoders in hardware.

Signal analysis & ML

Modulation and code recognition, emitter identification, SNR estimation, and spectrum monitoring.

Wireless & optical links

Visible light, cellular and satellite communications, reconfigurable surfaces, and localization.

Physical-layer security

GPS spoofing detection, secure cyber-physical systems, and true random number generators in CMOS.

Storage & reliable circuits

Coding for data storage systems and reliable circuit design, often in collaboration with circuit designers.

Radar & the contested spectrum

Cognitive radar — sensors that learn their environment and adapt their waveforms on the fly — and radar signal processing more broadly. I am equally drawn to the other side of the problem: what a receiver can infer about, and do against, the emitters it observes.

Most of this work happens through industrial R&D and consulting rather than in papers. It is also the subject of my new course, EE 481 Introduction to Radar Signal Processing.
